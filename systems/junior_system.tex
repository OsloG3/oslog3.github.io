\section*{Minor suit raise}
\label{sec:org8d72aab}
\subsubsection*{Inverted minors}
\label{sec:org9c2e0dd}
1-2 and 1-2 show an invitational or better raise without a 4 card or longer major. \\
Inverted minors continuations:
\begin{itemize}
\item New suits without jumps show stoppers, and are bid up the line.
\item Jumps to new suits show shortness.
\item Bidding NTs indicates that we think we have all suits stopped.
\end{itemize}
Bidding 2 or 3 of the opened minors are always not forcing. By responder it shows he only had an invitational raise, and by opener it shows he would not accept an invite. \\

1m - 3m show a less than invitational raise and deny a side 4 card major. The hand should be strong enough to want to be in game opposite 18-19 balanced. 
\section*{Major suit raises}
\label{sec:org0bcd976}
\subsubsection*{Simples raise}
\label{sec:org98289f4}
1M-2M shows at least 3 card support and 5-10 points. \\
Opener can bid a new suit to show values in the bid suit and interest in game opposite a maximum (8-10 points) 
\subsubsection*{Mixed raise}
\label{sec:orgcb5c9c0}
1M-3M shows 4 card support, 6-9 points and not a flat hand (4333 or a 4432 with a two honors in the doubleton) 
\subsubsection*{Maas Raise}
\label{sec:orgd4f034f}
1M-2 shows invitational or minimum (10-14 points) values 3 or more cards support. \\
Opener can bid 3M with a minimum to reject the invite or 4M if accepts the invite with no hope of slam. \\
\subsubsection*{2/1 delayed raise}
\label{sec:orgff4807d}
When partner opens a major and we have 14/15+ points and a fit we start by bidding a new suit. \\
\section*{Other responses to 1 level openings}
\label{sec:orgc81ad36}
\begin{itemize}
\item New suits at the 1 level show 4 or more cards and are forcing 1 round.
\item New suits at the 2 level without jumps show a hand two over one.
\item Single jumps to a new suit show 6+ cards in the bid suit and are invitational. They deny side 4 card majors and support for partner's suit
\item Double jumps to new suits are splinters.
\item 1m - 1 shows 5-10 points and no suit biddable at the 1 level
\item 1m - 2 is invitation balanced without 4 card majors
\item 1M-1 shows 5-11 points without support for the opened majors, 1-1 also denies 4 or more spades.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{2/1 auctions}
\label{sec:org97edb32}
1-2 and 1-2 are natural and promising 5 or more diamonds.\\
1-2 similarly promises 5 or hearts and is \\
1-2, 1-2 and 1-2 are and could be natural or they could be from a balanced hand with no better bid.\\

After a 2/1 response opener describes his hand naturally, but he can not bid above 2 of the opening suit unless he has 15+ points.\\

\subsubsection*{Fourth suit }
\label{sec:org3e8d840}
When opener bids a second suit at the 2 or higher level then bidding the remaining unbid suit by the responder is game forcing and asks for more information about the opener’s hand.\\

1 - 1\\
2 -? \\

1 - 1\\
2 -? \\
… \\

Bidding the fourth suit (the last remaining unbid suit) is artificial and game forcing. It asks if opener has 3 card support for responder’s suit or a stopper in the fourth suit. \\
With no 3 card support nor a stopper in the fourth suit and no other descriptive bid to make opener can rebid his opening suit: waiting and showing no better bid. \\

\subsubsection*{After we overcall at the 1 or 2 level without jump}
\label{sec:orge9e54bc}
\begin{itemize}
\item New suits at the 1 level are forcing for 1 round
\item New suits at the 2 level are not forcing but show a constructive or invitational hand
\item New suits at the 3 level are both with and without a jump
\item 1/2 are both natural showing a stopper in the opponents’ suit. 1 shows 8-11 points, 2 after a 1 level overcall shows 12-14 points and 2 after a 2 level overcall show 9-11 points.
\item Cuebidding opponents' suit without jump shows either an invitational or better support or a balanced hand with enough strength to play game but no stopper in opponents suit.
\item Jump cuebidding shows a mixed raise (4card support 6-9hcp and some distribution)
\end{itemize}
